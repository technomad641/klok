# Design: Energy-aware klok: the daily review and the weekly digest

## Problem Statement
People doing knowledge work don't know which work drains them or when they are at their best, so they schedule their hardest work at their worst hours and burn out without seeing why. klok already records what you did, how you felt (on its mindful side), and when. But those two live apart, the check-ins are too much work to sustain, and klok never tells you what the combination means. As a plain time tracker, klok competes with mature tools (Timewarrior, Watson, timetrap, Toggl) and wins nobody. The feelings-to-work link is the one thing none of them have.

## What Makes This Cool
klok becomes the only tracker that can answer "what kind of work drains me, and when am I actually at my best?" from your own private, local, plaintext data, with no screen surveillance, no cloud, no account. The payoff is the first weekly digest that tells you something true about yourself you half-suspected but never proved: "Code reviews cost you about 1.3 energy points on average, while deep work on your main project lifts you." (Whether focus fades across a long unbroken stretch is a rougher, experimental read that comes later, once there is enough rated data to trust it; it is not what the first slice leads with.) Deterministic, offline, yours.

## Constraints
- Zero runtime dependencies, Python stdlib only, `requires-python >= 3.9`. Adding a dependency is a deliberate design change.
- Single-record data model (`Frame` in `frames.jsonl`); mindful data in `checkins.jsonl`. New capture must stay plaintext and hand-editable.
- Adoption dies on check-in friction. Capture must be near-zero-effort or the digest has no data.
- Insights must be honest: never report a correlation sparse data cannot support.
- Ships via the existing channel (PyPI `klok-cli`, GitHub). No new distribution.

## Premises (all agreed)
1. Pure time-tracking is commodity; the feelings-to-work link is the only thing worth building around. Tracking is substrate, not pitch.
2. Target user: a knowledge worker or freelancer who cares about energy, focus, and burnout and keeps a light habit. Not the billing user.
3. Adoption lives or dies on check-in friction; near-zero-effort capture is core, not a nice-to-have.
4. Payoff is a plain-language weekly digest of correlations klok actually found, deterministic and offline, with optional LLM phrasing later.
5. No new distribution problem; the product is new commands plus a better digest.

## Approaches Considered
### Approach A: Digest-first (minimal)
`klok insights`/digest over existing data plus one-keystroke check-ins. Smallest ship. Rejected as the first slice: too thin on sparse data, so the digest reads weak or misleading before enough check-ins exist.

### Approach B: Full energy-aware loop (ideal)
Capture prompts everywhere, an insight engine (analyzers with effect size, sample count, confidence), a weekly digest, and a dashboard upgrade. This is the destination, but too big for a first ship and prone to over-building analyzers nobody reads.

### Approach C: Daily-review ritual + digest (chosen)
A roughly 20-second end-of-day `klok review` that walks the day's tracked blocks and asks one quick energy rating per block (a digit then Enter), backfilling feelings cheaply; the weekly `klok digest` is built on the dense, clean data that ritual produces.

## Recommended Approach
Approach C. Premise 3 says the whole thing dies on friction, so the first thing to build is the capture mechanism that survives real use, not analytics on data you do not have. The end-of-day review is one habit instead of four daily interruptions, it produces the clean dataset the digest needs, and it is the most distinctly klok thing on the table. It grows straight into B: the analyzers written for the digest become the insight engine, and in-the-moment prompts can be added later as a second capture path.

### What to build, in order (first slice)
1. **Capture, `klok review [DATE|RANGE]`.** Walk the day's tracked frames that lack an energy rating, in order, printing `project +tags  duration  (start-stop)` and asking for a 1 to 5 energy rating typed then Enter (blank line skips, `q` quits, `b` goes back), read through an injectable input callable on `Context` so the in-process test suite can drive it (the same spirit as the `sleep=`/`clock=` seams in focus/mindful). It reuses the existing line-based `_ask`/`isatty` path rather than new raw-terminal code (D3), so it is cross-platform and testable; the pitch is "about 20 seconds," not literally one keystroke. Write one `Checkin` per rated block, with its `energy` set and timestamped at that block's start instant, so the join below associates it with that block by time (no frame-id field is added; the model stays as-is and hand-editable). Reuse `CheckinStore` and `checkins.jsonl`; check-ins live in their own file and so never appear in frame-based client reports.
   - **Scope.** By default review walks the active sheet only (`--all-sheets` to include every sheet), skips practice frames (the dashboard's `is_practice`: breathing/meditation), and skips a currently-running frame (it is rated on the next review after it is stopped).
   - **"Already rated" means energy specifically.** A block is skipped only when a check-in inside its interval has a non-null `energy` value. A pre-existing mood- or stress-only check-in (where `energy` is `None`, which `klok checkin` can write) does not mask an unrated block.
   - **Re-rating replaces, never duplicates.** `b` (go back) and `--redo` update the block's existing energy check-in in place rather than appending a second one.
   - Also accept the fast inline forms `klok stop --felt 4` and a `klok checkin 4` energy-only shorthand.
2. **Join.** Extract the dashboard's timestamp-based pairing (`frame_at`, and `is_practice`) into one small reusable, tested helper module so `dashboard`, `digest`, and `review` share it without `insights` importing `dashboard`. `frame_at` is half-open (`start <= t < end`) and breaks ties by latest start, which is why review timestamps each check-in at the block's start instant: the reverse lookup then stays unambiguous even for adjacent or overlapping blocks. Both the review's skip/overwrite test and the analyzer consider only energy-bearing check-ins (`energy is not None`); mood/stress-only check-ins are ignored by the energy join. Extraction must preserve `klok dashboard` output exactly, with a regression test asserting it is unchanged (D4). `CheckinStore` has no `update`, so re-rating (`b`/`--redo`) is implemented as remove-then-add and gets its own test; it is new code, not free.
3. **Analysis, `insights.py` (first slice = ONE analyzer, D2).** A single deterministic analyzer: drain-vs-lift per project and per tag (mean energy of blocks carrying it minus the overall mean), returning a `Finding {headline, detail, effect_size, n}`. Concrete suppression rule: surface a project or tag only when it has `n >= 5` rated blocks AND its mean differs from the overall mean by at least a fixed effect size (start: 0.5 energy points); rank survivors by effect size. No per-hour-of-day or per-weekday buckets and no fade analyzer in the first slice (see the go/no-go gate in Next Steps): with roughly 80 ratings over two weeks, `n >= 5` across 24 hour-buckets or 7 weekday-buckets would suppress almost everything, and the honesty constraint beats the findings count. The `Finding` shape reserves room for a `confidence` field when the deferred analyzers arrive.
4. **Digest, `klok digest [:week|RANGE]`.** Print the surviving findings in plain language, ordered by effect size, with an honesty footer ("based on N rated blocks across D days; rate more with `klok review` for sharper results"), and write the same text to a file for sharing. Terminal first; the dashboard shows the same findings in the B phase.

Keep it stdlib: every statistic is a count, a mean, or a simple delta. No numpy, no ML.

## Open Questions
- Scale: reuse the existing 1 to 5 energy field only, or also quick mood? Recommend energy-only for the ritual to keep it one keystroke; richer check-ins stay on `klok checkin`.
- In-the-moment vs retrospective: the review rates in hindsight, which is less accurate. Acceptable for v1; add optional on-stop prompts in the B phase and compare.
- **Cross-sheet overlap (known limitation, from the pre-ship adversarial review).** Check-ins carry no sheet, while `review`/`stop --felt`/`digest` scope their frames to one sheet. When two blocks on different sheets overlap in time, a rating stamped at the shared instant is ambiguous: it can look like it rates the other sheet's block, and `--redo` can reassign it. After the field-preserving `replace` fix no user data is destroyed, so this is ambiguity, not loss. Proper fix (a sheet on the energy check-in, or a sheet-aware join) is a data-model change deferred past the first slice; concurrent cross-sheet overlapping blocks are the uncommon case this affects.
- Should `klok review` nudge proactively (a one-line prompt in `klok status` after the day's last block)? Recommend a gentle nudge, off by default via config.
- Minimum-data threshold and confidence model: start simple (n >= 5, report effect size) and tune once real data exists.

## Success Criteria
- A user who runs `klok review` once a day for two weeks gets a `klok digest` with at least one honest, specific project/tag drain finding and zero findings the data cannot support (more findings arrive with the gated analyzers; honesty beats count).
- The daily review of a normal day (6 to 10 blocks) takes under 30 seconds.
- Zero new runtime dependencies; every new analyzer unit-tested with synthetic frames and check-ins; full suite green on 3.9 to 3.13.
- Nothing lands in a client sheet; mindful data stays on its own sheet.

## Distribution Plan
Existing channel. New commands ship inside `klok-cli`; a minor release (1.2.0) bumps both version files, and a GitHub Release triggers the existing Trusted Publishing workflow to PyPI. Document `review` and `digest` in README and `docs/reference.md`. No CI changes beyond the existing matrix.

## Next Steps (build order)
1. Extract `frame_at` (and `is_practice`) into a small shared, tested helper so `insights`/`review` need not import `dashboard`; preserve `klok dashboard` output exactly, with a regression test asserting it is unchanged (D4).
2. Implement `klok review` (digit+Enter via an injectable input seam) plus `stop --felt` and the `checkin <n>` shorthand, with tests driving the real `main()`. Verify the `checkin` positional does not break the existing `--mood/--energy/--stress` parsing or the leading-dash `_preprocess` handling.
3. Implement the single project/tag drain analyzer in `insights.py` with synthetic-data unit tests (zero rated blocks, one block, all-equal energy, n below threshold) and the concrete suppression rule above.
4. Implement `klok digest` (terminal + file) with the honesty footer; wire into grouped help.
5. README + reference docs; bump to 1.2.0 (both version files); release.

**GO/NO-GO GATE (before any deferred analyzer).** The 5-day dogfood in The Assignment is the gate, not homework. Only after it returns at least one honest project/tag drain pattern in real data do the fade analyzer, the per-hour-of-day and per-weekday analyzers, the confidence model, and the dashboard view get built (the B phase). If the dogfood shows no honest pattern, stop and fix capture first rather than building analytics the data cannot support.

## The Assignment
Before building any of this, prove the insight exists in your own data. For the next 5 working days, track as usual, then at day's end spend 20 seconds rating each block's energy by hand (`klok checkin` or a scratch note). On day 5, eyeball it: is there one real pattern, a draining project or a best hour? If yes, the digest is worth building and you already know your first finding. If you cannot find a single honest pattern in your own week, the feature is premature and capture has to come first. Either answer tells you exactly what to build next.

## What I noticed about how you think
- You called the app "too simple" and said it does not "make a lot of sense." You were not asking for more features, you were asking what the thing is actually for. That is the right question, and most people never ask it about their own work.
- You shipped klok to PyPI, wrote a real README, and built a dashboard before deciding what problem it solves. You build first and interrogate later, and the review ritual plays to that: ship the capture, let the data tell you whether the insight is real.
- You said being "useful to a smaller set of people, that's also fine." Holding scope small on purpose is what makes this buildable. Most people widen until nothing ships.
